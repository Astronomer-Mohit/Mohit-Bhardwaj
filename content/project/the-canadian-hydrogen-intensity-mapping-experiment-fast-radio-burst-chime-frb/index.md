CHIME is an interferometric radio telescope that has no moving parts. Originally conceived to map the most abundant element in the universe—hydrogen—over a good fraction of the observable universe, this unusual telescope is optimized to have a high "mapping speed", which requires a large instantaneous field of view (~200 square degrees) and broad frequency coverage (400–800 MHz). The digitized signals collected by CHIME will be processed to form a three-dimensional map of hydrogen density, which will be used to measure the expansion history of the universe. At the same time, these signals can be searched for fast, transient radio emission, making CHIME a unique telescope for discovering new fast radio bursts and for monitoring many pulsars on a daily basis.

The CHIME telescope's large collecting area, wide bandwidth, and enormous field of view make it a superb detector of FRBs. As of mid-2020, CHIME had detected well over 1,000 fast radio burst sources. This high event rate promises major progress on this puzzling astrophysical phenomenon. Bright CHIME-discovered FRBs can be found in real time and reported to the worldwide astrophysical community for multi-wavelength follow-up.
